# The Iced Mate Latte — and the Golden Vanilla Recipe Worth Making Twice

The Mate Latte was designed for a mug. Hot water, a whisk, thirty seconds,
something warm to hold while the day starts. That is the version most people
meet first, and it is the version the pouch describes.

Then summer arrives, or you make it at two in the afternoon instead of eight in
the morning, and you put it over ice out of curiosity. And it turns out the cold
version is not a compromise. For a lot of people it becomes the default.

This is a short piece about why that happens, what actually changes when you drop
the temperature, and one recipe worth making properly — Golden Vanilla Mate, with
honey, vanilla and cinnamon, over ice.

---

## What changes when it gets cold

Temperature is not a neutral thing you do to a drink. It changes what you taste.

**Cold mutes bitterness and lifts sweetness's cousins.** Yerba mate has a green,
slightly grassy edge — the thing people either love immediately or take a week to
come around to. Chilled, that edge steps back. It is still there, still
recognisably mate, but it stops being the first thing you notice.

**Cold pushes the vanilla forward.** The Mate Latte is a vanilla latte, and
vanilla is an aromatic that reads differently at different temperatures. Hot, it
rounds the drink off. Cold, it reads cleaner and more distinctly — closer to the
vanilla in ice cream than the vanilla in a pastry.

**Cold changes the body.** Over ice, with the milk poured separately, the drink
layers before you stir it. That is partly why an iced latte feels lighter than the
same ingredients served warm: the texture is doing something different, even
before the dilution starts.

None of this makes iced better in some objective sense. It makes it a different
drink from the same pouch — which is the useful part. One tin, two habits.

---

## The practical case for iced

There is a less romantic argument, and it is probably the real reason people
switch.

**It works at the wrong time of day.** A hot drink at three in the afternoon is a
commitment. An iced one is not. The Mate Latte carries 100mg of natural caffeine —
roughly a cup of coffee — alongside 500mg of Lion's Mane and 200mg of L-Theanine,
and the whole formula is the same whatever temperature you drink it at. The
difference is whether you actually want it at three o'clock. Iced, most people do.

**It survives being forgotten.** A hot drink you walk away from is a cold drink
you do not want. An iced drink you walk away from is an iced drink.

**It is still thirty seconds.** Nothing about the preparation gets harder. One
scoop, a little hot water to dissolve it properly, milk, ice. The hot water step
is not optional and it is the one people skip — more on that below.

## Golden Vanilla Mate

This is the version worth making when you have two extra minutes: the Mate Latte
with honey, vanilla and cinnamon, poured over ice and milk. It tastes like
something you would pay eight dollars for and wait in line to collect.

### What you'll need

- 2.5 tbsp Milonga Vanilla Mate Latte
- 2 oz hot water
- 6 oz oat or almond milk
- Ice
- 1 tsp honey
- ¼ tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of cinnamon
- Optional: cinnamon stick for garnish

### How to make it

**01 — Make the mate.** Add 2.5 tbsp Milonga Mate Latte to 2 oz hot water. Stir
until completely dissolved.

**02 — Make it creamy.** Add 1 tsp honey, ¼ tsp vanilla, and a pinch of cinnamon.
Stir well.

**03 — Pour over ice.** Fill a glass with ice and pour in 6 oz of your favorite
milk.

**04 — Finish.** Pour the Mate Latte over the milk. Give it a gentle stir and top
with a light dusting of cinnamon.

## The three things that decide whether it works

Most iced lattes that come out disappointing fail in the same three places.

**One: dissolve it in hot water first.** This is the step that matters most and
the one that gets skipped, because it feels like an unnecessary detour on the way
to a cold drink. It is not. Powder does not fully dissolve in cold milk — it
disperses, which is a different thing. You get a drink that tastes right for the
first two sips and then leaves a gritty inch at the bottom of the glass. Two
ounces of hot water, thirty seconds of stirring, and the whole glass tastes the
same as the first mouthful.

**Two: sweeten while it is hot, too.** Honey in cold milk is honey in a stringy
lump at the bottom of the glass. Honey stirred into hot mate is honey that has
disappeared into the drink. Same for the vanilla and the cinnamon — the
aromatics open up in warm liquid and then stay open once it is cold.

**Three: pour the mate over the milk, not the other way round.** This is purely
aesthetic and it is worth doing anyway. Milk first, ice already in the glass,
then the mate poured slowly over the top — you get the layered pour before you
stir, which is the reason anyone photographs an iced latte at all.

## Variations, if you want them

The recipe above is the one to learn first. Once it is in your hands, it moves
easily.

**Skip the honey** if your milk is already sweetened — most barista oat milks are,
and the two together can tip it over.

**Swap the cinnamon for cardamom** if you want it less Christmas and more chai.
A very small pinch; cardamom does not need much.

**Make it a double.** Two ounces of hot water is enough to dissolve 2.5
tablespoons comfortably. If you want it stronger, add powder rather than removing
water — dissolving is the bit that needs the liquid.

**Blend it.** Ice, milk, dissolved mate and honey in a blender is a frozen version
that works better than it has any right to. Less elegant, more fun.

## The honest summary

The Mate Latte is 15 servings, 90 calories and 3g of sugar a serving, and none of
that changes whether you drink it hot or cold. What changes is where it fits in
the day. Hot, it is a morning drink, and a good one. Cold, it turns into the thing
you make at two in the afternoon when you want something to look forward to
rather than something to get through.

The Golden Vanilla version takes it one step further — honey, vanilla, cinnamon,
and a slow pour over ice. It is four steps. It is still ready in about the time it
takes to find a glass.

Same scoop. Completely different drink.
